Optimized Local Housing

The shortest total walk the beds allow.

A Timberborn 1.1 mod. Once a day, it moves adult beavers into the homes that make the colony's total walk to work as short as it can be. Beds are limited, so not everyone lives next door to work. Together, they walk the least.

  • The whole colony, not each beaver. One beaver may walk a little farther so that everyone together walks less.
  • Every home keeps its adults, and children never move. Breeding is untouched. Empty beds are left to the game.
  • Once a day, spread over many ticks. Nothing to set, no menu. It needs no other mods.

Homes shouldn't be a lottery.

By the look of its code, the game hands out beds without asking where a beaver works. Beavers end up walking across the map every day, past homes right beside their job.

The real question is which arrangement of beavers into beds makes the total walk smallest. That is a classic assignment problem. This mod solves it exactly, once a day, instead of nudging beavers one swap at a time. It only rearranges the adults already housed.

How the plan is drawn up

Six rules the mod keeps every time it arranges your colony.

  • Optimal, not first-come-first-served

    Each district is solved exactly with the Hungarian algorithm. The colony's total commute is the smallest the rules allow.

  • Real routes, not straight lines

    Homes are priced with the game's own route cost, so stairs, platforms and ziplines count. Each workplace checks real routes to the 32 nearest homes in its district.

  • Families and breeding stay safe

    Every home keeps exactly the same number of adults, and children never move. Beds for babies stay where they are.

  • No pointless shuffling

    Beavers move only when the rearrangement saves at least one route-cost unit for each beaver moved. Once a colony is arranged, another pass changes nothing.

  • Smooth by design

    The work is spread over many ticks, a little at a time, so there is no long freeze. A large colony does more per tick to finish within the daytime; only the very largest take longer.

  • Built with co-op in mind

    Every player's game should make the same moves, even across a save, a reload or a mid-pass join. What has been played

One pass, five steps

The first pass starts as soon as the game runs on a save the mod hasn't seen. After that, a pass runs at the start of every day.

  1. Capture

    Every housed adult beaver, its home and its assigned workplace, in one quick snapshot.

  2. Price

    Real route costs from each workplace to its 32 nearest homes in its district. Farther homes are estimated.

  3. Solve

    The best way to reassign the adults to the beds adults use today, one district at a time.

  4. Verify

    Every proposed move is priced again with fresh routes. A cycle of moves is dropped if it would cut a beaver off from work, or if it saves too little.

  5. Apply

    Whole cycles of moves are applied in a single tick, and rolled back if the game refuses any of them.

What it always does

  • Leaves unemployed adults free to give up good beds to beavers who commute.
  • Repairs a broken commute by moving a beaver to a home that can reach its workplace, when a bed can be arranged.
  • Skips a move if the beaver, its job or its home changed while the pass was running, and tries again the next day.

What it never does

  • Move anyone into a paused, blocked or automation-off home, or out of one.
  • Move a beaver who can reach work into a home that can't.
  • Move a beaver into another district.
  • Leave a beaver homeless.

What is tested, and what isn't

Stable means the evidence below, not that every situation has been tried.

Tested

  • 16 passes in a row in a live game (about 350 adults, 104 homes, 167 workplaces) with no errors, warnings or rollbacks, on the code the stable release started from.
  • A hosted co-op session with a second player on the same version: about 11,000 ticks, 15 passes, and no desync in the host's log.
  • 41 automated checks: 40 run on every change, and one runs against the installed game. They cover the solver against brute force, several districts, large colonies, and two players staying in step through saves and reloads.
  • The replay of a real colony above.

Not yet

  • This release's newer changes in a live game: pricing homes by district, remembered route costs, solving each district on its own, and bigger budgets for large colonies. Automated tests cover them.
  • Frame-time impact: the per-tick work is bounded, but a real path query hasn't been timed, nor the bigger budgets a very large colony gets.
  • Multiplayer beyond that one hosted session: joining, rehosting, the second player's side, and a live two-player session on this release.
  • The Iron Teeth faction (developed and replayed on a Folktails colony).
  • Operating systems other than Windows.
  • Timber Together: its code was read against this mod's, but not played together.

Five minutes to install

  1. The ZIP under Assets on the release page, not Source code.
  2. Close the game and extract it into Documents\Timberborn\Mods: one OptimizedLocalHousing folder.
  3. Enable it and restart. Not alongside Housing Optimize or Commute Balancer.
